### 2. Does the thesis show scientific originality? — **+ (criteria met)**

The genuinely original move is the *inversion*: turning a yield/mission-time simulator into a solver for the largest tolerable spectral noise amplitude at a fixed 90th-percentile mission time (main.tex:621–624, 733). LIFE II already assumes the photon-noise-limited regime and states that "Appendix A presents a preliminary analysis of the aforementioned instrumental effects and constrains the maximum level of allowed perturbations to operate in this fundamental noise limited regime" (Dannert et al. 2022, §2.3.2, p. 7) — so constraining instrumental noise against the photon floor is not new. Expressing that constraint as a *wavelength-shaped equivalent single-output rate whose amplitude is set by completion time of the whole Experiment-1 program* is, on the material supplied, not published. The gap statement at main.tex:206 ("They do not yet provide the wavelength-dependent iso-mission-time boundary for a mechanism-independent aggregate random-noise term") is therefore, as far as I can verify, accurate — and carefully enough hedged that it does not overclaim.

The local-zodiacal normalization finding is a second, independent piece of originality and it is **real, not an artifact** — I verified it directly. `git show 2fd5142:lifesim/instrument/pn_localzodi.py` contains, at the taper branch, `ap = np.ones_like(self.data.inst['radius_map'])` for `fov_taper == 'gaussian'` (LIFEsim's default) versus `ap = np.where(radius_map <= image_size/2, 1, 0)` for `'none'`, followed by `lz_flux = lz_flux_sr * (np.pi * image_angle**2)` and `lz_leak = (ap*t_map).sum(...)/ap.sum() * lz_flux * area`. The default branch therefore divides a circular-solid-angle prefactor by a square pixel count, and the *same file's other branch* uses the self-consistent circular mask. The π/4 suppression and the 4/π = 1.2732 correction claimed at main.tex:607–611 and 975–984 are correct, and the internal-inconsistency argument makes "bug, not convention" airtight.

What blocks a higher tag is that the thesis contains **no related-work discussion at all**. Novelty rests on the single sentence at main.tex:206 and is never defended against (a) Dannert 2025, whose title is literally *How Quiet Is LIFE? Performance of Nulling Interferometers under Instrumental and Fundamental Noise* and which is cited eight times but never characterised; (b) Norbruis 2023, which built a LIFEsim-derived model, took P-Pop populations as a test set, and ran grid + single/multi-objective optimisation over array geometry, aperture area and instrument temperature to produce Pareto fronts (Norbruis 2023, Summary; ToC §5.2–5.4, §6) — i.e. the same genre of LIFEsim-based trade-space exploration; (c) Binkert 2023. Both prior group theses do exactly what this one omits: Binkert has §6.2 "Comparison to Previous Work"; Norbruis has §2.3 "State of the art" with §2.3.4 "Research gap". Having read both, I can confirm this thesis is **not** a repackaging of either — Binkert is signal extraction / ML / spectral-dip detection (no overlap at all), and Norbruis optimises geometry against a detection figure of merit rather than inverting for a noise allowance. But the thesis cannot demonstrate that, because it never tries.

### 3. Are there new ideas or established ideas used in a new way? — **o (noteworthy but no grade impact as presented)**

This is the weakest of the four, and the reason is specific: after subtracting what is already published and what the student did but left out of the document, the remaining "new idea" content is thin, and the one idea placed most prominently is the weakest of the set.

**The uniform-disk Bessel average is a re-derivation of a published result, presented without attribution.** Lay (2005, *Appl. Opt.* 44, 5859), §3.2 "Stellar Leakage", Eq. (17), p. 5866 gives the leakage from a resolved uniform disk as `N* = Σ_j Σ_k 2F* A_j A_k cos φ_jk · J₁(2πB_jkθ*/λ)/(2πB_jkθ*/λ)`, with the small-angle expansion `≈ ΣΣ 2F* A_j A_k cos φ_jk [1 − 0.125 (2πB_jkθ*/λ)²]`, and states "The leakage rate increases with the square of the nulling baseline lengths." That is precisely the `2J₁(x)/x` structure the thesis derives at main.tex:930/936 and expands to `x²/32` at main.tex:944–945. The thesis cites only the NIST DLMF (main.tex:926, 1093–1099) for the Bessel identities and never points at Lay's Eq. (17), even though `lay2005imaging` is already in the bibliography and the student's own copy of the paper is annotated. Any LIFE-collaboration reader will recognise it immediately.

**The null-order/throughput trade is likewise published and uncited.** Guyon et al. (2013, *PASP* 125, 951), §1, p. 952: "for a fixed number of apertures, a smaller fraction of the total light gathered by the interferometer is used as null order is increased. For example, in the 4-aperture Angel Cross design, 25% of the light is used (only one of the four interferometer outputs offers the θ⁴ null), while a simpler 2-aperture Bracewell offers 50% throughput with a θ² null." The thesis states the identical physics as a self-imposed caveat of its own ansatz (main.tex:488, 811, 856) without noting that the trade is an established design constraint. Guyon 2013 and Laugier 2020 are both in the student's literature folder and **neither appears in the bibliography** (main.tex:1031–1101 has 11 entries).

**Consequence for the `sin^n` ansatz.** It is not a "known trick" as such — but it is a *less* general substitute for machinery the student already had. Lay 2005 Eq. (2)/(6)–(8) gives an exact baseline decomposition of the chopped response for an arbitrary combiner via `cos φ_jk`, and Guyon 2013 / Laugier 2020 supply real θ⁴ and kernel-nuller designs. The obvious examiner question — "why an unnormalized `sin⁴φ` envelope instead of Lay's decomposition applied to an Angel-Cross or kernel-nuller combiner?" — has no answer anywhere in the text. The limitation itself *is* honestly and repeatedly stated (main.tex:472–488 Table "Interpretation boundary of the null-order proxy"; 616; 811; 856; 868; 884), and this honesty is the thesis's strongest methodological virtue. But honesty about a limitation is not the same as a positioned methodological choice.

**What genuinely does count here, and two items are missing from the document.** (i) The `u = 1/λ` wavenumber substitution to de-chirp the transmission×Planck bin integral (main.tex:967–973; `transmission_analytic.py:22–57`) is a clean and correct application of an established quadrature idea at a place it had not been applied, and the fact that the same substitution logic removes chirp at both the spatial and spectral integration stages is elegant. (ii) The general even-order reduction `sin^{2p}φ → binomial cosine series → Σ_j J₀(j k r)` (main.tex:589–596, Appendix A Eq. "sine power series") genuinely extends Lay's second-order-only expression and is what makes the order-4 runs possible at all. (iii) *Not in the thesis*: the result that odd null orders average identically to zero under a full rotation, giving a physical reason why null order is always even — present only in `transmission_analytic.py:8–13` and `ANALYTIC_NOISE_REWRITE.md` §12. (iv) *Not in the thesis*: the TSE exploits monotonicity to warm-start the 2D scans (`trade_space_explorer.py:438–453`, `upper_limits[j:][upper_limits[j:] > cutoff] = cutoff`), which is a real algorithmic contribution; main.tex:624 describes only the bracketed exponential-fit search, and main.tex:626 discusses monotonicity without saying it is *used*.

### 4. Are the results of the thesis novel and important? — **o (noteworthy but no grade impact as presented)**

**The headline reversal is a two-step corollary of published facts, not new insight.** LIFE II establishes the background hierarchy explicitly: "at short wavelengths the stellar leakage dominates strongly, while for longer wavelengths the emission from the local zodiacal dust contributes most to the shot noise" (Dannert et al. 2022, §2.4, p. 7, describing Fig. 5 top panel). Guyon 2013 §1 establishes that higher-order nulls suppress leakage on partially resolved disks. Remove the short-λ leakage floor and the cheapest place to hide added variance moves to long λ. The thesis effectively concedes this: it pre-announces the reasoning at main.tex:420 ("Typically, a star emits more strongly in the shorter wavelengths, so we might add some margin to the shorter wavelengths…") and disposes of the explanation in one paragraph at main.tex:807–809. Calling it "an initially counter-intuitive reversal" (main.tex:206) is therefore mild salesmanship. What *is* new and worth stating is the non-obvious part: that the naive spectral ordering survives intact through the nonlinear chain of per-target SNR → detection campaign → top-50 selection → four follow-ups + characterization → 90th percentile over 100 universes, and does so in both catalogs and in the full two-endpoint contour scans (main.tex:704, 728, 804). That claim is genuinely the thesis's own and is properly evidenced.

**The central causal claim is explicitly not established.** main.tex:809: "no ablation run isolates leakage suppression from the simultaneous extended-source and planet-throughput changes." Since the proxy simultaneously changes the far-field envelope average from 1/4 to 3/16 (a 25% extended-source reduction, main.tex:811, 820) and the planet response, the order-4 numbers in Table \ref{tab: budget shapes} (main.tex:686–693) mix at least three effects. The honest statement is present; the missing run is one AMS campaign away and is the single cheapest thing that would upgrade this block.

**Transportability of the numbers is low and the thesis says so.** Ramp endpoints have unequal spectral integrals and are not a cost ranking (main.tex:696, 733); values cannot be interpolated (main.tex:702); they are search point estimates with no defensible error bar (main.tex:624, 868); the 90th percentile's 84–96 rank interval cannot be converted to years from the retained products (main.tex:535, 859); scheduler strictness and empty-universe omission bias in opposite directions with unknown net sign (main.tex:533). All correct, all self-reported. The result is a defensible impact study, exactly as the thesis calls it at main.tex:873.

**The most transportable result is under-sold.** The 4/π local-zodiacal correction is the one finding a LIFE collaborator would act on tomorrow: it applies to every LIFEsim run at the default `fov_taper='gaussian'`, and local zodi dominates the long-wavelength shot noise (LIFE II Fig. 5). The thesis never states (a) that the defect is pre-existing upstream LIFEsim code and not introduced by this work, (b) the smoking-gun evidence that the same file's `'none'` branch is self-consistent while only the default branch is not, or (c) whether previously published LIFE yields are affected. It only says "arose from a geometric inconsistency in the removed pixel-grid path" (main.tex:607) and "This is a normalization correction, not a new dust model" (main.tex:611). That is an **underclaim**, and the strongest evidence sits in an internal brief rather than in the graded document.

**Framing check on the "bridge quantity".** main.tex:208 ("The result is therefore a bridge quantity. It translates a science-level completion time into an allowed single-output random count rate at a defined pre-efficiency reference plane") is standard requirements engineering — an equivalent input-referred noise allowance at a declared interface. To the thesis's credit it does **not** claim this framing as novel, and the reference-plane bookkeeping is made unusually explicit (Table at main.tex:351–369). The LIFE-specific content is the *choice* of reference plane and the iso-mission-time criterion, not the concept. Minor issue: "bridge quantity" is a self-coined term never related to the standard vocabulary, which will read as invented jargon to an instrument engineer.

Correct, validated to ~1e-16 against a 2×10⁶-sample brute rotation average (main.tex:548), and a real extension of the second-order-only published expression. 4. **The inversion framing itself** — solving for a noise allowance at fixed mission time rather than reporting a yield at fixed noise (main.tex:621–624, 733) — which is a genuine reorientation of the group's standard workflow and directly serves the Instrument Science Team's requirements process.
