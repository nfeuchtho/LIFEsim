# Examiner Report — Block: Independent scientific thinking / originality

**Thesis:** *Iso-Mission-Time Random-Noise Error Budgets for LIFE with Arbitrary Nulling and Noise Models* (N. Feuchthofen, IPA / Quanz Group, July 2026), `thesis/main.pdf`, 71 pp.

**Scope of this report:** ETH grading-sheet criterion "Independent scientific thinking / originality" only. Scientific competence, methodological competence, presentation and work process are covered by other examiners and are deliberately not graded here, even where evidence for them is visible.

**Independence statement:** This evaluation was formed from `thesis/main.pdf`, the published literature in `Master Thesis/Literature/` (LIFE I/II/III/VI, Guyon et al. 2013, Lay 2004, Lay 2005, Laugier et al. 2020, Kennedy et al. 2015, Dannert PhD thesis, Norbruis and Binkert MSc theses), and spot-checks of `lifesim/`. ## 1. Verdict

This is a thesis with a real, identifiable, independent contribution that is nevertheless smaller than the framing in Section 1.2 implies. The student's own work is concentrated in four places: (i) the discovery and diagnosis of a 27.3 % local-zodiacal normalisation defect in inherited LIFEsim code (Sec. 4.2, Eq. 4.5, App. A.4), together with the demonstration that it moves the derived allowance by factors of 1.5–11 (Table 4.1); (ii) the recognition, from his own reproduction runs, that an iso-mission-time requirement is pathologically sensitive because it is defined where the completion curve is steep — a 10⁻⁴ model perturbation producing a 10⁻¹ requirement change (App. B.3, Sec. 6.3); (iii) the observation that a design that is *worse on the median target* completes the programme *sooner*, because a mission observes the top of the target distribution and not its median (Sec. 5.7, Table 5.3); and (iv) the AMS/TSE software layer that makes the repeated inversion affordable at all (Sec. 3.5, 3.6, 6.3). Points (ii) and (iii) are genuine scientific ideas, not engineering. Point (i) is a genuine finding about a tool the group publishes with.

Against that, the two most prominently advertised novelties do not survive contact with the literature at the scope claimed. The "transmission model that takes an interferometer's aperture positions and beam-combiner matrix" (Sec. 1.2, third of "four things new"; Eq. 3.4) is, as an equation, Lay (2004) Eq. (3) — a paper the thesis itself cites as [9] — and in matrix form Guyon et al. (2013) Eq. (1)/(5). Only the *cost* claim attached to it is new. The "iso-mission-time inversion itself" (first of the four) is closer to Dannert's Chapter 5 (Fig. 5.11, Table 5.1: 5 yr and 10 yr mission-time contours, including 90 % mission-success contours, over an instrument-parameter plane, with requirement values read off them) than Section 1.2's description of the predecessor admits. The bolded structural claim about four apertures and chopping is over-generalised from a single 2D example and does not engage the TPF-I/Darwin architecture-trade literature in which the question was already live.

The student is, to his credit, unusually disciplined about what his numbers do *not* mean (Sec. 5.3, Table 6.1, Sec. 6.4, conclusion p. 60). That restraint is itself evidence of independent scientific judgement — but it also means that, by the author's own accounting, the numerical results are not yet important. Under this criterion the work reads as a competent, honest, partly original methods thesis whose most valuable outputs are a cautionary methodological result and a bug fix, not a new mission requirement.

## 2. Findings by sub-question

### 2.1 How significant is the independent contribution of the student to the outcome of the thesis?

**(+) The local-zodiacal normalisation defect is unambiguously the student's own finding, and it is consequential.**
Section 4.2 (pp. 34–36) and Appendix A.4 (Eq. A.13/A.14) identify that the inherited grid path multiplied a *square*-domain average (Ω_square = 4R²_FoV) by a *circular* solid angle (Ω_circ = πR²_FoV), suppressing the uniform foreground by π/4. The evidence offered is internal and checkable — the untapered branch of the same routine normalised by a circular mask while the default tapered branch normalised by the square pixel sum, so the two branches disagreed on the same physical configuration (p. 35). The corrected circular normalisation is what the current code carries (`lifesim/instrument/pn_localzodi.py:123`). The quantified consequence (median one-hour SNR down 9.2 % at order two, 9.8 % at order four for Hab2Max; Table 4.1: derived flat allowance changing by 5.3×, 1.6×, 11.0×, 1.5×) is a substantive result about a tool the group publishes with. Finding a normalisation error of this kind inside inherited, working, published-adjacent code, and then proving it is a defect rather than a modelling choice, is exactly the kind of independent contribution this criterion asks about.

**(+) The sensitivity/amplification result (App. B.3, Sec. 6.3, p. 55) is an original methodological finding, arrived at by the student's own reproduction discipline.**
Running the reference array through the general-combiner path instead of its closed form changes mission time by 0.012 % and median SNR by 1.6 × 10⁻⁴, yet moves the Hab2Min 8-year short-weighted endpoint by 11.9 % (App. B.3, p. 68). The generalisation drawn — "a model difference of order 10⁻⁴ produces a requirement difference of order 10⁻¹ ... a background correction should be treated as invalidating [such requirements] rather than perturbing them" (p. 55, p. 68) — is a transferable caution for the whole LIFE requirements programme and is not, to my knowledge, stated anywhere in the LIFE series. It was found by rerunning his own work rather than by being told to look.

**(+) The "top of the distribution, not the median" argument (Sec. 5.7, p. 50; Table 5.3; Sec. 6.1, p. 53) is the best genuinely scientific idea in the thesis.**
The six-aperture design has median one-hour SNR 0.93 (Hab2Max) and 0.90 (Hab2Min) of the reference, and only ~28 % of targets improve at all — yet it reaches zero-budget completion in 4.98 yr against 5.32 yr. The resolution offered (the best fifty targets, which are the nearest and most resolved and therefore the ones geometric leakage limits, improve by 1.22/1.20) is non-obvious, correct, and cleanly evidenced. It is also a genuinely new way to read an architecture trade for LIFE: prior comparisons (e.g. Lay 2005, Table 3) rank configurations by modulation efficiency and stellar leakage, i.e. by per-target metrics, not by where in the target distribution the schedule is actually set.

**(+) The two-sense disambiguation of "agnostic" (Sec. 2.6, p. 19) and the explicit definition of the pre-efficiency reference plane (Sec. 1, p. 7; Table 2.4) are the student's own conceptual hygiene.**
These are not results, but they are the kind of definitional care that makes a bridge quantity usable by an engineering team, and they are not inherited.

**(-) A large fraction of the substrate is inherited and the thesis operates it rather than extends it.**
The source model, SNR convention, chopping/modulation formalism and P-Pop interface are LIFE paper II [2] and Dannert [7]; Experiment 1, the SNR 7 / 44.1 thresholds, the five-epoch scheme, the greedy scheduler and the 90th-percentile mission-time metric are all inherited (Table 2.1, Sec. 3.6.2); the Hab2Min/Hab2Max catalogues are Kammerer et al. via Bryson et al. [4,6]. Section 1.2 states this honestly, which is to the student's credit — but the criterion asks for the size of the delta, and the delta is a wrapper (AMS), a search (TSE), one analytic reduction, one bug, and two architectures.

**(-) The generality that is built is almost entirely unexercised, so much of the claimed contribution is potential rather than demonstrated.**
Equation 3.1 defines multiplicative *and* additive modifiers over four channels — "up to eight free functions" (p. 20) — and the thesis then uses the additive branch only (p. 22: "The condensed study reported here varies additive background noise only"). Equation 3.4 admits an arbitrary combiner and the thesis evaluates two designs (Sec. 4.3, p. 37: "two designs are not a survey"). The functional budget space is sampled by one flat and two linear families (Sec. 5.2, Eqs. 5.1–5.3), whose ramp endpoints are conceded to be "imposed shape parameters, not independently inferred wavelength limits" (p. 41) and which "do not provide independent limits at individual wavelengths" (p. 45). Building general machinery and then reporting a razor-thin slice of it is a legitimate scoping decision, but it caps how much of the machinery counts as delivered contribution.

### 2.2 Does the thesis show scientific originality?

**(+) The reframing of the requirement question — from "is the instrument fundamental-noise-limited?" to "what added noise still completes the science programme in the allotted time?" — is a better-posed question than the predecessor's, and choosing it was the student's call.**
Dannert's criterion in his Chapter 3 is a *local* ratio test (instrumental vs. fundamental noise per wavelength bin; his Fig. 3.7 and Tables 3.4/3.5), and in Chapter 5 the fundamental-noise limit is defined by ∂log(τ)/∂log(σ) < 1 (his Eq. 5.3). Both are proxies. Tying the allowance directly to the completion time of a stated science programme is a cleaner formulation, and Section 2.6 (p. 19) argues for it explicitly and correctly (the N_I = 0 origin is "the *hardest* engineering requirement", not an easy design).

**(+) The 1D similarity reduction (Eq. 3.5, Sec. 3.5) is a small but genuinely clever step and it is what makes the whole architecture-agnostic claim affordable.**
Recognising that for a geometry scaling with a single baseline (r_k = b u_k) the rotation-averaged response of *any* combiner depends on the target only through x = πbθ/λ, and can therefore be tabulated once per design, converts an O(image_size²) per-target grid into a 1D interpolation. The measured outcome (14.3 s general vs. 14.7 s closed form, p. 25; agreement with the analytic Bessel reduction to 6 × 10⁻¹²) is a real result. The idea is not deep, but it is the student's and it is load-bearing.

**(o) The extension of the azimuthal average to arbitrary even null order (Eq. 4.2, App. A.1) is correctly and voluntarily bounded.**
Appendix A.2 (p. 62) states plainly: "The second-order result is not new: Eq. A.5 applied to the double-Bracewell response reproduces the uniform-disk stellar leakage given by Lay [10]. What the derivation above adds is the extension to arbitrary even null order ... and its reuse inside the one-dimensional radial quadrature." That is the correct attribution and the correct size of claim. I note it as (o) rather than (+) because the general-order coefficient sum is a routine binomial expansion plus the standard DLMF Bessel integral; the originality lies in noticing it was worth doing, not in the mathematics.

**(-) The most prominently advertised novelty is scoped too broadly and the precedent is in the thesis's own bibliography.**
Section 1.2 (p. 9) lists as the third of "four things new": "a transmission model that takes an interferometer's aperture positions and beam-combiner matrix rather than a fixed analytic response". Lay (2004), cited in this thesis as [9], Section 2, states: "we derive the response on the sky for an arbitrary phased array of collectors", and his Eq. (3) is R(s) = |Σ_j A_j exp(i(φ_j + 2π x_j·s/λ))|² — Eq. 3.4 with A_j e^{iφ_j} → U_jk. Guyon et al. (2013) [11] make the matrix and its SVD structure explicit. Section 3.3 itself does not claim the equation as new, and Table 3.1 credits Guyon for the 50 %/25 % throughput figures, so this is a framing failure rather than a misattribution — but in the one place a reader looks for the novelty claim, the claim is stated at a scope the literature does not leave open. The defensible claim is narrower: *evaluating* an arbitrary combiner inside the LIFEsim source model at the cost of the closed form.

**(-) The relationship to Dannert's Chapter 5 is under-described in a way that flatters the delta.**
Section 1.2 (p. 8) characterises the predecessor as: "the predecessor propagates an assumed instrumental noise level forward to a yield". Dannert's Fig. 5.11 in fact sweeps aperture diameter against perturbation rms and draws *mission-time contours at 5 yr and 10 yr, including 90 % mission-success contours*, then reads requirement values off them (his Table 5.1; "To stay fundamental noise limited, the OPD perturbations need to stay below rms(OPD) < 0.35 nm and amplitude perturbations below rms(δa) < 0.02 %"). His Chapter 3 additionally produces "sets of allowable noise levels" (his Tables 3.4, 3.5) and already observes that "the requirements are much less strict in the longer-wavelength regime" — which is the qualitative content of this thesis's short-weighted-ramp result at null order two. The genuine delta remains: an *aggregate mechanism-agnostic* term rather than per-mechanism rms values; a *wavelength-resolved shape family* rather than a scalar; a bracketed root-find rather than a grid; and dependence on the *architecture*. That delta is real and worth stating — but it should have been stated against what Dannert actually did.

**(-) The bolded four-aperture/chopping constraint is over-generalised, and its precedent is not engaged.**
Section 3.4 (p. 24) concludes in bold: "**A fourth-order null at four apertures is therefore incompatible with the chopping scheme LIFE relies on**", repeated in the conclusion (p. 59). Two problems. First, the argument is demonstrated for one 2D design, the Angel Cross, whose single deep output is already stated by Guyon et al. (2013): "in the 4-aperture Angel Cross design, 25 % of the light is used (only one of the four interferometer outputs offers the 4th null)". Second, Guyon's own 1D result — "Any 1D interferometer with N apertures can achieve a 2(N−1)-order null" — means a *linear* four-aperture array has outputs at orders 0, 2, 4 and 6, so the mode-counting behind "a four-aperture array admits only one fourth-order output" does not transfer without argument. The claim as generalised is asserted, not derived. Separately, the requirement that a chop be formed from an antisymmetric pair of nulled outputs is the kernel-null condition of Laugier et al. (2020) [12] (their Sec. 3.1: conjugate pairs of nulled outputs produce an antisymmetric map); [12] is cited only as "the natural family in which further designs would be expressed" (p. 25) and in the outlook, never where it would have supplied the general form of the argument.

**(-) The six-aperture "double triple nuller" is a composition of two textbook elements, and the TPF-I/Darwin architecture-trade literature is not engaged.**
The 1:−2:1 triple is Angel & Woolf's degenerate Angel cross (reachable from Guyon [11], which the thesis does cite for the 2(N−1) rule); the ±π/4 recombination of two sub-nullers along the imaging baseline is LIFE's own scheme. The construction is sound and cleanly implemented (`lifesim/util/combiner.py:385–414`; the three specified rows are orthonormal, so the design is embeddable in a lossless combiner), but it is an assembly of known parts and is presented without reference to the eight-configuration TPF/Darwin trade of Lay (2005), Table 3, which includes three-way nulling designs ("Linear-3", "Triangle") evaluated for exactly this purpose. Lay (2005) is in the thesis's bibliography as [10] but is used only for the rotation-independence of symmetric sources and the uniform-disk leakage. A referee would ask for that comparison.

### 2.3 Are there new ideas, or established ideas used in a new way?

**(+) Established ideas used in a new way — this is where the thesis is strongest.**
Three examples, each anchored: (a) the standard Bessel azimuthal average, known for the double Bracewell since Lay, is generalised to even order 2p and then *reused inside a 1D radial quadrature* for tapered and extended sources (Eq. 4.2 → Eq. A.11), which is what removes image size as a production-science parameter (Sec. 4.2, Fig. 4.2); (b) the arbitrary-combiner response, known since Lay 2004, is combined with the single-baseline similarity variable to become as cheap as the closed form rather than more expensive (Eq. 3.4 + Eq. 3.5); (c) the 90th-percentile mission time, inherited as a *reporting* statistic, is turned into the *root* of a search (Sec. 5.1). None of the ingredients is new; each recombination is.

**(+) The 1/N normalisation convention (Sec. 3.3, p. 24) is a small original piece of reasoning with real consequences.**
"Throughput therefore ceases to be a separate quantity requiring its own bookkeeping: it is the number of outputs used for science divided by N ... holding throughput fixed while changing the response is exactly the error an idealized shape model invites." This is the student thinking about what makes a cross-architecture comparison honest, and it is what licenses the discussion on p. 53 that an idealised comparison "overstates the fourth-order allowances by a factor of several and places the feasibility boundary in field of regard some fifteen degrees too far." That is an original and useful negative result about how *not* to do this comparison.

**(-) The genuinely new idea count is low for a Master's thesis at this length.**
Stripping out the re-application, what remains as new *ideas* (as opposed to new numbers or new code) is: the amplification caution, the top-of-distribution reading of the architecture trade, and the 1/N throughput-honesty argument. That is three, and only the first two are load-bearing for the thesis's own conclusions.

**(o) The "sin^n proxy replaced by a real combiner" step is the right methodological instinct.**
Section 6.1 (p. 53) is explicit that holding throughput, area and baseline prescription fixed while deepening a null "describes no realizable instrument", and that only the *ordering* of the budget families survives the substitution, not the magnitudes. Recognising and reporting that one's earlier proxy was misleading is a mark of independent judgement; I record it without evaluative weight here because the corrected calculation is what is graded, not the correction.

### 2.4 Are the results of the thesis novel and important?

**(+) Novel, within their stated scope.**
The eight-row Table 5.1 and the four-row Table 5.3 are, as far as I can determine, the first wavelength-resolved iso-mission-time aggregate random-noise allowances published for LIFE, and the null-order reversal of the least-damaging spectral family (short-weighted at order two, long-weighted at order four, without exception in eight rows, and confirmed off-axis by the 2D contours of Fig. 5.3) is a new statement. The integral-neutrality check (p. 42: a ramp of endpoint A has mean A/2, so the neutral reference is twice the flat amplitude; the winning family exceeds it in all four rows at each order) is the student's own guard against the result being a reporting artefact, and it is the right guard.

**(-) Importance is weak, and the thesis establishes this itself.**
The reported amplitudes are conditional on a fixed inherited operating point that is not optimised (Table 2.1: fixed 4 m collectors, η = 0.09, R = 20, SNR 44.1 as a "retained broadband proxy", limiting magnitude 7, FoR 65°, 12 h slew). They are *charged* time, not calendar time — Sec. 3.6.2 notes the 5.5 yr target "would then correspond to roughly 6.9 years of elapsed mission", so the allowances "are correspondingly optimistic if the reader interprets the target as elapsed time." They carry a search tolerance of order 20–80 ph s⁻¹ μm⁻¹ (App. B.3) against reported values as small as 62. And by the thesis's own amplification result they cannot be transferred between calculations "that differ even slightly in the underlying transmission" (p. 68). The conclusion states the position correctly: "These allowances do not yet constitute subsystem requirements" (p. 60). Under the criterion's word *important*, that is a self-assessed no.

**(-) The single most important finding is deliberately left unexploited.**
Section 4.2 (p. 35) states: "the present work does not attempt to propagate it into any previously published LIFE yield number. Doing so would require rerunning those studies and is left to future work." A 27.3 % error in what Section 6.1 (p. 53) identifies as "the dominant contribution" to the background near 10 μm (local zodiacal supplying ~1760 of 2260 ph s⁻¹ μm⁻¹ at order two) is precisely the kind of finding whose importance is realised by propagating it. Leaving it unpropagated is a defensible scoping decision for a Master's thesis, but it means the finding's importance is asserted rather than demonstrated.

**(-) The architecture-dependence result rests on n = 2.**
Sec. 4.3, p. 37: "two designs are not a survey"; Sec. 6.4, Table 6.1, Priority 1: "The requirement is shown to depend on the array, but only two designs are evaluated, and total collecting area is held equal so that aperture count and collector size do not trade against each other." Sec. 6.5 correctly identifies the needed next step (a kernel-nuller survey with the equal-area convention relaxed). The result as it stands — *that* the requirement is array-dependent — is worth knowing but is close to a priori expectation once one accepts that leakage scales with null order.

**Publishability.** In part, and as a component rather than a standalone paper. Concretely: (a) the analytic background reduction, its validation matrix (Table 3.2) and the local-zodiacal normalisation correction with its quantified SNR impact would form a solid methods/errata section of a LIFEsim-successor instrument paper, and are the part most likely to be *cited*; (b) the amplification caution (App. B.3, Sec. 6.3) is a short, publishable methodological note that any group deriving mission-time-based requirements should read; (c) the null-order/spectral-preference reversal plus the top-of-distribution argument could support an A&A Letter or a section of a LIFE architecture paper — but a referee would require the survey of Sec. 6.5, engagement with the TPF-I architecture trade, and calendar-time rather than charged-time reporting before accepting the requirement numbers. Nothing here is publishable as a standalone refereed paper in its present state.

## 3. Best achievement

Finding, diagnosing and quantifying the local-zodiacal normalisation defect in inherited LIFEsim code, and then following it through to its methodological consequence. The chain is: an internal inconsistency between two branches of the same routine (Sec. 4.2, p. 35) → a π/4 solid-angle mismatch with a derived correction factor and its untapered limit vs. realised value 1.267 (Eq. 4.5, App. A.4) → a measured median SNR shift of 9.1–9.8 % (p. 35) → derived allowances changing by factors of 1.5 to 11 (Table 4.1) → the general lesson that iso-mission-time requirements amplify background errors because they are defined where the completion curve is steep, confirmed independently with a 10⁻⁴ perturbation in App. B.3. That chain is the student's own from end to end, it corrects a real defect in a tool the group uses, and it produces a transferable caution rather than just a fixed number. It is worth more than the headline table.

## 4. Opportunity for improvement

State novelty at the scope the literature actually leaves open, and read the neighbouring literature before claiming the gap. Three of the four items in the Section 1.2 novelty list needed narrowing: the transmission model is Lay (2004) Eq. (3) and Guyon (2013) Eq. (1) — only its *cost inside this pipeline* is new; the inversion is a wavelength-resolved, aggregate, root-found version of something Dannert's Fig. 5.11 already does with grids and per-mechanism rms values; and the chopping constraint needed either the general kernel-null argument from Laugier (2020) or an explicit restriction to 2D four-aperture arrays, plus a citation of the TPF/Darwin architecture trade (Lay 2005, Table 3) in which three-way nulling configurations were already compared. The underlying work would have been no smaller and the claims would have been unassailable. A related, smaller point: the six-aperture design should have been positioned relative to the existing Darwin/TPF-I configuration zoo rather than introduced as a fresh construction.

## 5. Suggested grade

### **5.00**

**Justification against the scale wording.** The scale offers "5.5 — very good, only minor flaws" and "5 — good, certain flaws". This is the latter, not the former, because the flaws under *this* criterion are specific, nameable, and go to the substance of what is new rather than to presentation of it: one of the four advertised novelties is a 2004 published equation restated (Sec. 1.2 item 3 vs. Lay 2004 Eq. 3); a second is described against a predecessor whose Chapter 5 already produced mission-time contours and requirement values (Sec. 1.2 vs. Dannert Fig. 5.11/Table 5.1); a bolded structural conclusion is generalised beyond the single example that supports it and past the relevant prior art (Sec. 3.4 vs. Guyon 2013 and Laugier 2020); the general machinery that justifies the title is exercised on two architectures, one modifier branch, and three one-parameter shapes; and the headline numbers are, by the author's own analysis (Sec. 3.6.2, App. B.3, Table 6.1), not transferable requirements. Any one of these would be a minor flaw. Together they are "certain flaws".

It is not 4.75 or lower, because the criterion is met and comfortably so: there is a real independent finding of consequence (the local-zodi defect and its propagation), a genuinely original methodological result (the requirement-amplification caution), a genuinely original scientific reading of the architecture trade (top-of-distribution rather than median), and a substantial and correctly reasoned software contribution (the 1D similarity reduction that makes an arbitrary combiner as cheap as the closed form). The student also demonstrates independent scientific judgement in the negative direction — recognising that his own earlier idealised-response comparison would have overstated the fourth-order allowances "by a factor of several" (p. 53) and reporting it — which is a form of originality the sheet's other criteria do not capture.

It is not 5.25, because raising it would require either that the results be *important* in the sense the criterion asks — and the thesis itself declines to claim that (p. 60: "These allowances do not yet constitute subsystem requirements"), leaves its most consequential finding unpropagated into the published yields it affects, and rests its architecture claim on two designs — or that the novelty claims be tight, which they are not.
