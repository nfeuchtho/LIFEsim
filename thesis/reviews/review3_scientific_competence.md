## 1. Verdict

This is a scientifically competent, unusually well-documented, and mostly correct piece of work whose central weaknesses lie not in its mathematics but in the interpretation of its own architecture comparison. Every analytic result I could check independently is right: the even-power/Jacobi–Anger reduction of Eq. 4.2 (including the factor ½ that the imaging cosine contributes after azimuthal integration, which is easy to get wrong), the uniform-disk limits x²/32 and x⁴/256, the x²/8 leakage ratio, the far-field values ¼ and 3/16, the 4/π solid-angle correction, the orthogonal-output structure of the Angel Cross, the 1:−2:1 triple, the baseline constants 0.5894 and 1.1376, and the 95.57 % binomial rank coverage. Numerical provenance is, where it exists, excellent: I traced roughly twenty distinct quantitative claims — Table 2.3's catalog inventory, every entry of Table 4.1, every entry of Tables 5.1/5.3/B.2, and all sixteen numbers in Section 5.4 — back to raw catalogs or reproducibility TSVs and they reproduce exactly. Table 3.2 and Table 6.1 are the two best pages of the thesis: a validation matrix that states what each check *cannot* establish, and a limitations table ranked by consequence rather than by remediability. That is a rare and mature instinct.

Against this stand four real problems. First, the equal-collecting-area comparison that carries the headline architectural result is not implemented as declared: the field of view is left at that of a 4 m collector while the six-aperture design necessarily has 3.27 m collectors, so its dominant background — local zodiacal light, ~86 % of the fourth-order total near 10 µm — is under-counted by up to a factor 1.5. This is an étendue effect, it is the classic penalty of subdividing a fixed collecting area, it is not mentioned anywhere, and it is large enough to erase the reported 6 % advantage. Second, the Discussion repeatedly mixes the idealized sin⁴ proxy with the realizable double triple nuller; the statement that a fourth-order null makes the requirement "about a quarter of the reduced background" is computed with an allowance the thesis itself disowns on p. 53, and the corresponding realizable figure is ~6 %. Third, Sections 5.6 and 6.1 give mutually contradictory far-field averages (1/6 vs 3/16) for the same comparison. Fourth, the literature base is thin — fifteen references, with neither Bracewell (1978) nor Angel & Woolf (1997) cited even though the thesis uses "Bracewell", the "Angel Cross" and the 1:−2:1 triple throughout, and with essentially no engagement with the Darwin/TPF-I instrument-error-budget literature that is the direct prior art for its own title. A duplicated paragraph on p. 55 states the same claim twice with two different magnitudes ("months" vs "years" of computer time).

The candidate demonstrably knows how to derive, how to validate, how to bound a claim and how to say what a result does not show. What is missing is the last increment of scepticism applied to the one comparison the thesis most wants to be true.

## 2. Findings by sub-question

### 2.1 Does the candidate show sufficient familiarity with the literature?

**(+) Citations are used precisely and for the right thing, not decoratively.** Lay is cited twice for two genuinely different points — Lay (2004) for the rotation-independence of the stellar and local-zodiacal geometric leakage rates and for the exclusion of systematics from the model boundary (Sec. 2.3, Table 2.5), Lay (2005) for the modulation/imaging argument (Sec. 2.3) and for the second-order uniform-disk leakage that Eq. A.5 reproduces (App. A.2). Guyon et al. (2013) is used correctly for the one-dimensional 2(N−1) null-order rule at N = 3 and for the 50 %/25 % throughput figures of Table 3.1 (Sec. 3.4). Kennedy et al. (2015) supports the exozodi surface density actually implemented (`pn_exozodi.py:84–88`, σ₀ = 7.11889 × 10⁻⁸, α = 0.34). The NIST DLMF is cited with specific equation numbers (10.9.1, 10.6.6a, 10.2.2), which is more rigour than most papers manage.

**(+) Section 1.2 is an exemplary statement of inheritance.** It says exactly what is taken from LIFE paper II (response, extraction formalism, source model), exactly what is taken from Dannert (2025) (the aggregate instrumental photon-noise term, and the explicit disclaimer "the aggregate random-noise quantity used here is not a new object, and the present work does not claim it as one"), and it positions the work against Norbruis (2023) and Binkert (2023) with a correct statement of the difference (yield optimisation vs. tolerance inversion at fixed design). Few Master's theses draw this boundary honestly.

**(−) The base is narrow: 15 unique references, 34 citation calls in 71 pages.** For a thesis on nulling-interferometer error budgets this is materially thin.

**(−) Founding references for the architectures actually studied are absent.** "Bracewell" appears throughout (Sec. 2, Sec. 3.2, Table 3.1, `combiner.double_bracewell`) with no citation of Bracewell (1978). The "Angel Cross" of Table 3.1 and the 1:−2:1 triple nuller of Sec. 3.4 are both due to Angel & Woolf (1997), which is not cited; only Guyon et al. (2013) is. Laugier et al. (2020) is cited for kernel nullers but Martinache & Ireland (2018), the origin of the concept, is not.

**(−) No engagement with the Darwin/TPF-I error-budget literature, which is the direct prior art.** The thesis's subject is an instrument noise allowance for a mid-IR nuller. That problem was worked in detail for TPF-I/Darwin, and Lay (2004) — the one paper of that lineage cited — is used only to *exclude* systematics, not to compare frameworks. There is no Defrère, Absil, Mennesson, or Cockell/Léger. Section 6.1 quotes "the instrument must be quiet to within a few percent of an irreducible foreground" without asking whether Darwin-era budgets reached a comparable conclusion.

**(−) No LIFE instrument or technology reference.** A thesis deriving requirements to be "allocated to concrete subsystems" (Sec. 6.5) cites no LIFE hardware, testbed or instrument-concept paper, though such material (e.g. Glauser et al., SPIE 2024) sits in the supplied literature folder. LIFE papers IV/V are also absent, despite the characterization threshold SNR 44.1 being an inherited broadband proxy for spectral retrieval performance (Table 2.1) — precisely the subject of those papers.

**(o)** The thesis is transparent that its stellar scaffold and η⊕ normalisation come from different populations and that Table 2.3 counts must not be read against LIFE VI yields (Sec. 2.2). That is the right caveat, correctly placed.

### 2.2 Are the aims/hypothesis/questions clearly formulated?

**(+) Yes, and unusually explicitly.** Section 1.1 states one central question in italics and decomposes it into five sub-questions, each of which is answered as a labelled bullet in Chapter 7. The reader is never in doubt what is being asked.

**(+) The two senses of "agnostic" are disambiguated before they can cause trouble** (Sec. 2.6): mechanism-agnostic (a modelling choice about what N_I means) vs. computationally agnostic (a parameter whose variation does not force SNR recomputation). This distinction is genuinely needed because the title uses one sense and the whole of Chapter 3 uses the other.

**(−) The central question asks for "instrument-agnostic requirements" and the thesis answers that no such thing exists.** Sec. 5.7 and the Conclusion state that the requirement depends on the array and "not only in magnitude". This is an honest and interesting negative result, but it means the headline question is answered in a weaker form than posed; the reframing from "instrument-agnostic" to "mechanism-agnostic" is handled in Sec. 2.6 rather than in Sec. 1.1 where the question is set.

**(−) "Within a reasonable mission time" is never defined, and the unit is not the one the reader assumes.** Sec. 3.6.2 states clearly that every reported time is *charged* time, and that at the configured 0.8 efficiency the 5.5-yr target corresponds to ≈6.9 yr elapsed. The disclosure is exemplary; but the question's operative phrase then floats free of any stated criterion, and Sec. 5 never returns to it.

**(o)** "Under pessimistic assumptions on the physical limits" is only partly delivered: the three agnostic parameters (mag 7, FoR 65°, 12 h slew) are argued to be conservative (Sec. 5 preamble), but throughput 0.15, QE 0.60 and 4 m collectors are the nominal inherited values, not pessimistic ones, and Sec. 2.1 says so.

### 2.3 Are the methods and techniques properly described?

**(++) The derivation is reproducible from the text alone, which is exactly what an appendix like A is for.** I re-derived Eq. 4.2 independently from Eq. A.1 and the Bessel representation A.2 and obtained the same coefficient sum including the ½ prefactor, whose origin (the odd cross-term ⟨sin²(z cos φ)·sin(2rz sin φ)⟩ = 0 under φ → −φ) is stated correctly in App. A.1 as "the complementary imaging phase contributes one half after full azimuthal integration". Eqs. A.6–A.10 follow and I confirmed each numerically.

**(+) The scheduler is described as an algorithm, not as a black box.** Sec. 3.6.2 gives six numbered steps, Eqs. 3.6–3.8, and — importantly — states the two boundary behaviours that bias the result in opposite directions (strict ≥51 detections in ≥91 universes; empty universes omitted). I verified both against `ams.py`: `time_sheet` is built on `np.unique(cat_det.nuniverse)`, so universes with no detection never enter the percentile, and `t_orbit`/`t_char` implement Eqs. 3.7/3.8 verbatim. The admission that "their net effect is not known from the retained outputs" is the correct thing to say.

**(+) Table 3.2 is a validation matrix, not a validation claim.** Each row states what the check establishes *and what it cannot*: the strongest agreement (4.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ against the shared source routine) is explicitly labelled as narrow because both routes share modules. The closing paragraph of Sec. 3.6.3 states that nothing validates the face-on exozodi assumption, the scheduler, or the fourth-order proxy as a realizable instrument. This is precisely the distinction between validation and self-consistency that the criterion asks about, and the candidate makes it unprompted.

**(−) Which model produced which number is not stated where it matters.** Table 4.1's "null order 4" rows (zero-budget 4.47 yr, flat allowance 641) come from the idealized sin⁴ proxy — confirmed against `localzodi_defect_impact.tsv`, rows `hab2hi/4/corrected` → 4.467216 yr, 641.2476 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ — whereas Tables 5.1/5.3's order-4 rows come from the physical six-aperture combiner (4.98 yr, 138.1). The Table 4.1 caption does not say so. `background_context.tsv`'s order-4 rows are likewise the proxy, identifiable because the local-zodiacal ratio between orders is exactly 0.75000 (1318.4002/1757.8672), the 3/16 ÷ 1/4 of the analytic envelope, not the 1/6 ÷ 1/4 = 0.667 of the realizable design.

**(−) A duplicated paragraph on p. 55 (`main.tex` lines 991 and 993) states the same claim with two different magnitudes**: "would have taken months of computer time" and "would have taken years of computer time". This is not merely an editing slip; it is a quantitative claim about the value of the reduction, given twice and inconsistently, with no provenance for either.

**(o)** Section 3.1's V = M × F + A formalism is introduced with eight free modifier functions, then only the additive branch is used. This is stated, but the apparatus is heavier than the study needs.

### 2.4 Are the methods appropriate for the subject?

**(+) The analytic reduction is the right method and is correctly bounded.** Replacing a per-target, per-wavelength pixel grid by an azimuthal Bessel series plus one radial quadrature is the correct exploitation of the symmetry that rotation-averaging creates, and Sec. 4.2 states the exact condition under which it fails (inclined or asymmetric exozodiacal disks). App. A.3 makes the same point for the general radial profile.

**(+) The (positions, U) formulation of Eq. 3.4 is the right generalisation, and it is genuinely cheap.** The key observation in Sec. 3.5 — that for a geometry scaling with one baseline the rotation average depends on the target only through x = πbθ/λ, so one table serves every star and wavelength — is correct and is implemented as stated (`combiner.azimuthal_spline`, `combiner.signal_noise_tables`). I confirmed that Eq. 3.4 with the double-Bracewell U reproduces the closed-form T₃,₂/T₄,₂ maps to 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ absolute and returns an empirical null order of 1.999999.

**(+) The 1/N normalisation is the correct way to make different aperture counts comparable,** and the observation that this removes throughput from separate bookkeeping (Sec. 3.3) is right: I verified Σ_j T_j = 1 for any θ and ⟨T_j⟩ → 1/N in the far field (measured 0.2502/0.2498 for the four-aperture design, 0.1662 for the six). Crucially this means the six-aperture design *is* automatically charged its throughput penalty in the realizable runs — a subtlety the thesis states and that many comparisons of this kind get wrong.

**(+) The iso-mission-time inversion is a legitimate and useful framing** for turning a science-completion criterion into an engineering bound, and the amplification caveat that follows from it (Sec. 6.3: a requirement defined where the completion curve is steep inherits an amplified version of any background error) is a real methodological insight, demonstrated twice — once with a 27.3 % background change and once with a 10⁻⁴ model change (App. B.3).

**(−) The equal-collecting-area convention is declared but not implemented.** See §3, error E4. This is the most serious methodological finding in this report.

**(−) The planet photon-noise coefficient is an RMS where a Poisson variance requires a mean.** Eq. 2.4 uses n_i = √⟨T₄²⟩; the shot-noise variance contributed by the planet is set by the expected *counts*, i.e. by ⟨T₃⟩ + ⟨T₄⟩, not by the root-mean-square response. I confirmed the implementation (`ams.py`: `nz_grid = sqrt((tm4_0**2).mean(axis=1))`; `combiner.signal_noise_tables`: `n_grid = sqrt((T[b_out]**2).mean(...))`). By Jensen the RMS is the larger, so planet shot noise is over-counted. The convention is inherited from LIFE paper II and the thesis writes the formula out honestly, but it does not flag it as an approximation — and by the thesis's own amplification argument, few-percent model details are exactly what one cannot leave unflagged here.

### 2.5 Has the research been carried out carefully?

**(++) Provenance, where it exists, is of publication standard.** I verified against the primary data: Table 2.3's 729 925 and 486 244 planet rows and 4505 unique stars directly from the two catalog files; every number in Table 4.1 against `localzodi_defect_impact.tsv`; every amplitude in Tables 5.1/5.3/B.2 against `physical_architecture.tsv`; and all sixteen quantitative statements in Sec. 5.4 against `stageb_grids.tsv` (factors 1.21/1.86 for magnitude, 2.44/2.80 for FoR, 14.4/24.0 for slew, the constant 135 above magnitude 6.4, the 61° feasibility edge, 47°/51° at shortest slew, the 20 h limit, the 41.8 %/39.1 % feasible fractions, and the peaks 331/210/983/741). All exact. This is a level of traceability I would expect from a good paper, not a thesis.

**(+) The independent reproduction in App. B.3 is real and is used to expose a reporting error rather than to reassure.** The candidate reruns the search from a committed state, records the achieved mission time (which the production campaign did not retain), and then reports that Table 5.1's "65" is neither the nearest ten nor two significant figures of the reproduced 62.2 — recommending Table B.2 be preferred. Self-criticism of that kind is worth more than a clean table.

**(+) The cluster cross-check is a genuine independent test.** Reproducing six searches on different hardware, OS, worker count and numerical-library version to 2.6 × 10⁻¹²–4.1 × 10⁻¹¹ relative, given a time-tolerance stopping rule, is not automatic, and the thesis correctly says so.

**(−) The Discussion conflates the idealized proxy with the realizable design at two points, once contradicting itself.** See errors E2 and E3 in §3. These sit in the chapter where the physical interpretation is delivered.

**(−) The precision figure 6 × 10⁻¹² is attached to the wrong quantity.** See error E5.

**(−) The statistics carrying the central physical interpretation have no provenance.** The claims in Sec. 5.7/6.1 — median one-hour SNR ratio 0.93 and 0.90, "only about 28 % of targets improve at all", best-fifty improvement factors 1.22 and 1.20, best-five-hundred ≈1.15, lower half degrading to 0.85 and 0.80 — support the thesis's most interesting sentence ("a design that is worse on average completes the programme sooner"). No reproducibility file, log or script anywhere under `thesis/reproducibility/` or `lifesim/analysis/` produces them. The reference peaks "roughly 560 and 460" in Fig. 5.4 are, by the thesis's own admission, "read from the corresponding colour bars"; `stageb_grids.tsv` contains only the fourth-order grids, and 1–2 of each 225 cells are unrecovered NaNs, which is not mentioned.

**(o)** The local-zodiacal radial integral is truncated at the 1 %-taper radius `image_angle`, discarding ≈1 % of a uniform foreground. Negligible in isolation; not obviously negligible under the thesis's own amplification argument, and unmentioned.

### 2.6 Have the results been appropriately tested by statistical analyses and/or sensitivity tests?

**(+) The sensitivity coverage is broad and mostly well chosen.** Two catalogs × two mission-time targets × three budget families × two architectures for the endpoints; two 15 × 15 operating-point scans per catalog per architecture; a corrected-vs-uncorrected local-zodiacal ablation over the whole catalog (`localzodi_snr_shift.tsv`: median −9.15 % at order two, −9.78 % at order four, 16th–84th range −10.7 % to −2.7 %, exactly as reported); a spectral-resolution convergence study in four noise regimes (Fig. 4.2); an eight- vs sixty-four-point wavelength-quadrature convergence check; and a general-combiner-vs-closed-form model sensitivity (App. B.3).

**(+) The integral-neutral normalisation check is the right robustness test to have thought of.** Comparing a ramp endpoint against twice the flat amplitude removes the obvious objection that the ordering is an artefact of the reporting convention. I verified all eight rows: at order two the short-weighted endpoint exceeds 2 × flat in 4/4 rows; at order four the long-weighted endpoint does in 4/4 rows.

**(+) The 90th-percentile rank uncertainty is treated properly and its limits stated.** App. B.4's 0.9557 coverage for [X₍₈₄₎, X₍₉₆₎] reproduces exactly (Σ_{k=84}^{95} C(100,k)0.9^k0.1^{100−k} = 0.95569), and the thesis correctly notes that this is rank uncertainty under the adopted population model only, excluding occurrence-rate, stellar and instrument uncertainty.

**(−) Half of the integral-neutral check is inside its own error bar.** At null order two the margins above the neutral reference are +13.5, +82.5, +73.5 and +48.3 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹, against a search uncertainty that App. B.3 itself puts at 20–80 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ per amplitude — and the neutral reference, being 2 × flat, carries twice that. In three of four order-two rows the claim is not resolved. (At order four the margins +71, +102, +49, +197 against a smaller slope-derived tolerance are safe, so the order-four half of the claim stands.) The thesis asserts the result for all rows without noting the asymmetry.

**(−) No reported amplitude carries an uncertainty.** Table 5.1, Table 5.3 and the Conclusion give point estimates. App. B.3 establishes the scale of the search uncertainty and App. B.4 the rank uncertainty, but neither is propagated onto a single reported number, and the thesis states that this cannot now be done from the retained products. The consequence is that the reader cannot tell, for example, whether "140 for the reference and 138 for the triple nuller" is a difference at all — it is not.

**(o) A relevant sensitivity test exists in the repository and is not reported.** `lifesim/ams/ablation_throughput.py` and `ablation_throughput.tsv` run a Guyon-motivated throughput-penalised arm (0.075 against 0.15). Two results follow: the idealized order-four proxy has *no* positive allowance at any study target when charged half throughput (zero-budget time 7.65 yr against a 5.5 yr target), and at the targets where it is feasible the long-weighted ordering survives (Hab2Max 8.0 yr: flat 38.4, short 54.7, long 142.6; Hab2Min 11.0 yr: 48.5, 81.4, 154.2). The second is direct support for the thesis's central claim and is not claimed. The statement on p. 49 that "no ablation run isolates leakage suppression from the simultaneous extended-source and planet-throughput changes" is literally true but reads oddly next to an unreported throughput ablation.

### 2.7 Are previous studies and the strengths and limitations of the own work critically discussed?

**(+) Table 6.1 is a model of its kind.** Six limitations ranked by their ability to change the numerical allowance or its engineering interpretation, with the ranking criterion stated ("relevance to the thesis's central numerical and architectural claims, not ease of remediation"). The first entry concedes the central comparison is two designs and not a survey; the third concedes that correlated instability and calibration bias cannot be allocated from N_I and "may impose stricter requirements".

**(+) The strongest single admission in the thesis is on p. 53:** that holding throughput, area and the baseline prescription fixed while deepening the null "describes no realizable instrument, and doing so overstates the fourth-order allowances by a factor of several and places the feasibility boundary in field of regard some fifteen degrees too far". Reporting that your own earlier configuration was wrong by a factor of several, and by how much, is the behaviour the criterion is looking for.

**(+) The local-zodiacal correction is discussed as a warning rather than as a triumph** (Sec. 6.3): requirements derived this way "should be quoted with the background model that produced them, and a background correction should be treated as invalidating them rather than perturbing them".

**(−) The most damaging limitation is not in Table 6.1.** The field-of-view/étendue inconsistency (error E4) is absent from the ranked limitations, from Sec. 4.3, from Sec. 5.7's "two boundaries limit the comparison", and from the Outlook. The two boundaries that *are* named (equal area, two designs not a survey) are both real, but neither is the one that could reverse the sign of the headline result.

**(−) The proxy/realizable boundary is respected in Sec. 5.7 and then breached in Sec. 6.1.** Having warned on p. 53 that the idealized comparison overstates the fourth-order allowances, the thesis then states the fourth-order relaxation using precisely those overstated allowances (error E3).

**(−) The critical discussion of previous studies is limited to positioning, not to comparison.** Sec. 1.2 says correctly what Norbruis and Binkert did differently, but no result of this thesis is compared numerically against any prior result; the closest is Sec. 2.2's explicit refusal to compare Table 2.3 counts with LIFE VI yields, which is correct but leaves the work uncalibrated against the literature.

### 2.8 Are the results placed in a broader context?

**(+) Sec. 6.1 does the necessary scale-setting**, which many theses omit: an allowance in ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ means nothing until it is stated as a fraction of the background it is added to, and the thesis gives ≈3 % (Hab2Max) and 1.5 % (Hab2Min) at order two against a band-averaged background of ≈4300 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ (I obtain 4394 unweighted, 4374 bin-width-weighted, from `background_context.tsv`). The conclusion drawn — "the instrument must be quiet to within a few percent of an irreducible foreground, which is a demanding requirement" — is the right engineering statement to extract.

**(+) The mechanism behind the architecture result is explained rather than asserted** (Sec. 5.7, 6.1): the best fifty targets are the nearest and most resolved, hence most limited by geometric leakage; a mission observes the top of the distribution, not its median; therefore a design that is worse on average finishes sooner. That is a genuine physical insight, correctly reasoned.

**(−) The most consequential mission-level implication of the thesis's own numbers is never drawn.** With the corrected background the zero-budget (perfectly noiseless instrument) charged times are 5.32 yr for Hab2Max and 7.41 yr for Hab2Min (Table 4.1). At the configured 0.8 efficiency these are ≈6.6 and ≈9.3 yr elapsed, against a LIFE concept lifetime the thesis itself gives as "roughly five years" (Sec. 5 preamble). The direct implication — that under the less favourable occurrence scenario Experiment 1 as defined cannot be completed within the nominal mission lifetime *regardless of the instrument noise budget* — is a stronger and more useful mission-design statement than any of the allowances reported, and it is left on the table.

**(−) The result is not related to any external requirement.** The thesis says (Sec. 6.5, Ch. 7) that the aggregate allowance should later be allocated to subsystems, but never gives the reader a single point of comparison — no detector dark-current rate, no thermal-emission estimate, no instability figure from Lay (2004) or from LIFE technology work — against which "140 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ at a pre-efficiency single-output plane" could be judged plausible or hopeless. The bridge quantity is built and then not walked across even once.

### 2.9 Are suggestions made for subsequent research?

**(+) Sec. 6.5 is concrete, prioritised, and follows from the stated limitations rather than from generic ambition.** Five specific proposals: a kernel-nuller survey over aperture number in the Laugier et al. framework (the natural family, correctly identified); relaxing the equal-area convention so aperture count and collector size trade explicitly; non-strict completion criteria with failed universes retained and per-universe component times saved, which would permit bootstrap intervals in years; extension of the analytic framework to inclined/asymmetric exozodiacal disks; and allocation of the aggregate allowance to concrete subsystems at the same reference plane.

**(+) App. B.1 lists exactly what a future run should store** — per-universe times, selected target IDs, brackets and stopping reason, catalog and scheduler configuration, and the architecture as (positions, U) with its own baseline constant. This is an actionable handover, not a platitude.

**(−) The suggestions do not include the two checks this report shows are needed most:** propagating collector diameter into the field of view under the equal-area convention, and re-deriving the fourth-order background scaling for the realizable combiner rather than for the sin⁴ envelope.

## 3. Factual and physics errors found

**E1 — Duplicated paragraph with contradictory magnitudes. Confirmed. High confidence.**
p. 55 (`main.tex` lines 991 and 993) contains the same paragraph twice. One says the trade space "would have taken months of computer time", the other "would have taken years of computer time". *Checked against:* the thesis's own Sec. 6.3 cost figures — pre-rewrite ≈25 min per catalog pass at image size 512, "several thousand evaluations" — give ≈52 days at 3000 evaluations and ≈6 months at 10 000. "Months" is supported; "years" is off by roughly an order of magnitude and has no provenance.

**E2 — Internally contradictory far-field averages. Confirmed. High confidence.**
Sec. 5.6 (p. 49) and Table 5.2 state that the far-field response average "falls from 1/4 to 1/6, which is one over the aperture count in each case", and in the same sentence attribute the measured 0.750 local-zodiacal scaling to "exactly the ratio of the far-field averages of the underlying envelopes". 1/6 ÷ 1/4 = 0.667, not 0.750. Sec. 6.1 then states the far-field averages "quoted in Section 5.6" as "3/16 to 1/4". *Checked against:* my own derivation (⟨T₂ₚ⟩ far field = ½·C(2p,p)/4^p, giving 1/4 and 3/16 for the analytic envelope) and numerical evaluation of `combiner.rotation_average` (four-aperture 0.2502/0.2498, six-aperture 0.1662 — i.e. 1/4 and 1/6 for the *physical* designs); and against `background_context.tsv`, where the order-2/order-4 local-zodiacal ratio at 10.093 µm is 1318.4002/1757.8672 = 0.750000 exactly, identifying that run as the analytic sin⁴ proxy. The two sections describe two different models and are asserted as one.

**E3 — Section 6.1 states the fourth-order relaxation with an allowance the thesis elsewhere disowns. Confirmed. High confidence. Most consequential.**
"The second-order flat allowances therefore amount to roughly 3 % of the natural background for Hab2Max and 1.5 % for Hab2Min… At order four the same allowances are about a quarter of the reduced background, which is a far more comfortable one." *Checked against:* `background_context.tsv` (order-4 band average 2426 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ bin-width-weighted, consistent with the quoted ≈2350) and `localzodi_defect_impact.tsv` (order-4 corrected flat allowances 641.25 and 596.45). 641/2426 = 26 % — the quoted quarter — but 641 is the *idealized proxy* allowance. The realizable six-aperture values are 138.1 and 129.3 (`physical_architecture.tsv`, Table B.2), giving 5.7 % and 5.4 %. Since "the same allowances" refers back to 140 and 65, the sentence reads as a statement about the thesis's headline architecture and overstates its headroom by a factor ≈4.6 — the same factor p. 53 warns about.

**E4 — The equal-collecting-area comparison does not propagate collector diameter into the field of view. Confirmed by code inspection. High confidence on the fact; medium-high on the magnitude. This is the unstated damaging limitation.**
`instrument.py:92–93` sets `telescope_area = num_apertures · π(diameter/2)²` and `instrument.py:104–105` sets `hfov = wl_bins/(2·diameter)`; `image_angle` (line 111) and the Gaussian taper (line 450, and `radial_average_tm`/`radial_average_general`) all scale with `hfov`. `lifesim/ams/settings.yaml` fixes `num_apertures: 4`, `diameter: 4.0`, and neither `runner.py` nor `ams.py` changes them when an architecture is supplied. Consequently every run — including the six-aperture ones — uses the field of view of a 4 m collector. Under the declared equal-total-area convention (Sec. 4.3, Sec. 5.7: "six smaller collectors rather than six of the same size") the six collectors must be 4·√(4/6) = 3.27 m, whose diffraction field of view is 1.22× larger in radius and 1.50× larger in solid angle. The local-zodiacal photon rate is I·Ω_FoV·⟨T⟩·A_tot (`pn_localzodi.py:123`, `lz_flux = lz_flux_sr · π·image_angle²`), so it is under-counted by up to that factor. Local zodi is the dominant fourth-order background — 1320 of 1530 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ near 10 µm by the thesis's own Sec. 6.1. Restoring the correct étendue would raise the fourth-order background by roughly 1.4× and, in a background-limited regime where time scales with background, comfortably erase the reported 6.4 %/6.0 % advantage of Table 5.3. This is the classic penalty of subdividing a fixed collecting area in a single-mode interferometer; the thesis flags the area convention twice but never this consequence, and it is absent from Table 6.1.

**E5 — The "6 × 10⁻¹²" agreement figure is attached to the wrong quantity. Confirmed. Medium-high confidence.**
Sec. 3.5 and Ch. 7 state that "the tabulated background response agrees with the analytic Bessel reduction of Appendix A to a relative 6 × 10⁻¹²". *Checked against:* direct evaluation. The background path (`combiner.radial_average_general`) integrates the disk with a 200-node trapezoid, while the analytic path (`transmission_analytic.disk_average_tm`) does the radial integral exactly via the Bessel derivative identity; the two therefore agree only to ≈2 × 10⁻⁵ (consistent with 1/200² trapezoid error) at realistic field-of-view radii, and to ≈1 × 10⁻² at the largest radius I tested. The point-source *azimuthal* average agrees to a median ≈2 × 10⁻¹¹ (max 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰) over x ∈ [1, 30] — the right order of magnitude for the quoted figure, but not the quantity named. Given App. B.3's own demonstration that a 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ model difference moves endpoints by up to 12 %, a 2 × 10⁻⁵ inconsistency between the two paths is not obviously negligible and should not be described as 6 × 10⁻¹².

**E6 — The four-aperture/one-deep-output counting argument is stated more generally than it holds. Confirmed. High confidence.**
Sec. 3.4 (bold), Sec. 4.3 and Ch. 7 assert that "a four-aperture array admits only one fourth-order output" as "a consequence of counting orthogonal outputs" and "a constraint [that] follows from the combiner structure alone rather than from any simulation". The count is correct for apertures spanning the plane: vanishing amplitude to first order imposes Σ_k U_jk = 0 and the vector condition Σ_k U_jk **r**_k = 0, i.e. three complex constraints on a four-dimensional space, leaving one solution. For a *collinear* four-aperture array the vector condition collapses to one scalar constraint, leaving a two-dimensional deep subspace. *Checked against:* explicit construction on apertures at x = (−1.5, −0.5, +0.5, +1.5)b — the rows (1, −1, −1, 1)/2 and (1, −3, 3, −1)/√20 are orthonormal, orthogonal to the bright row and to the position vector, and `combiner.null_order` returns 4.000 and 6.000 for them. The thesis's *physical* conclusion for LIFE survives (a collinear array's outputs are all even in the sky coordinate, so no pair chops in LIFE's sense), but the genericity assumption is never stated and the claim is presented as a theorem.

**E7 — App. B.3's sensitivity percentages use an inconsistent baseline. Confirmed. Medium-high confidence.**
"Nine of the twelve agree within one percent, but the Hab2Max six-year flat value falls by 4.3 %, the Hab2Min seven-and-a-half-year flat value by 6.8 %, and the Hab2Min eight-year short-weighted value by 11.9 %." *Checked against:* `physical_architecture.tsv` (general-combiner reference run: 583.689, 60.574, 854.600) and Table B.2 (closed-form rerun: 610.3, 62.2, 973.9). Against Table B.2 the differences are −4.4 %, −2.6 %, −12.3 %. The quoted figures are reproduced exactly only when computed against the *rounded* Table 5.1 values 610, 65 and 970: −4.31 %, −6.81 %, −11.90 %. Since App. B.3 itself identifies "65" as a rounding error to be superseded by 62.2, the 6.8 % figure is inflated by an error the same appendix diagnoses.

**E8 — The integral-neutral robustness claim is unresolved at null order two. Confirmed. High confidence.**
Sec. 5.2: "the short-weighted endpoint exceeds the neutral value at null order two in all four rows". The endpoints do exceed 2 × flat, by +13.5, +82.5, +73.5 and +48.3 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ (from Table B.2). App. B.3 states the search uncertainty as 20–80 ph s⁻¹ µm⁻¹ per amplitude; the neutral reference, being twice the flat amplitude, inherits twice that. Three of the four order-two margins are therefore smaller than their own uncertainty. The order-four half of the claim (+71, +102, +49, +197 against a smaller slope-derived tolerance of ≈8–23) is sound.

**E9 — Planet photon-noise coefficient. Confirmed as implemented; medium confidence that it is an error rather than a deliberate matched-filter convention.**
Eq. 2.4 uses n_i = √⟨T₄²⟩ for a Poisson variance whose coefficient should be the mean transmitted rate ⟨T₃⟩ + ⟨T₄⟩. *Checked against:* `ams.py` (`nz_grid = np.sqrt((tm4_0 ** 2).mean(axis=1))`) and `combiner.signal_noise_tables` (`n_grid = np.sqrt((T[b_out] ** 2).mean(axis=-1))`). Since √⟨T²⟩ ≥ ⟨T⟩, planet shot noise is over-counted; the background terms, by contrast, correctly use rotation-averaged *means*, so the two are on different conventions. Inherited from LIFE paper II and transparently written out, but not identified as an approximation.

**E10 — No provenance for the statistics carrying the central interpretation. Confirmed. High confidence.**
The Sec. 5.7/6.1 figures 0.93, 0.90, "about 28 % of targets improve", 1.22, 1.20, ≈1.15, 0.85, 0.80 appear in no reproducibility artifact and are produced by no script in `lifesim/analysis/`. Every other numerical claim I attempted to trace resolved to a file; these do not, and they are the ones the physical argument rests on.

**Non-errors I checked and confirmed correct** (recorded so the candidate is not asked to re-defend them): T₃,₂ − T₄,₂ = sin²φ sin 2ψ; Eq. 4.2 including its ½ prefactor and Bessel argument 2πjbϑ/λ; Eqs. 4.3/4.4 and A.6–A.10 (x²/32, −x⁴/768, x⁴/256, ratio x²/8); far-field ¼ and 3/16; the 4/π = 1.2732 local-zodiacal solid-angle correction and its implementation; Angel Cross output structure (measured null orders 0, 4, 2, 2); the 1:−2:1 triple's 2(cos ψ − 1) amplitude and orthonormality of the six-aperture combiner rows; baseline constants 0.5894021 and 1.1375675; the "31 % of achievable signal" mis-sizing figure (measured 0.3154); Eq. 3.4 reproducing the closed form (3.5 × 10⁻¹⁵) and returning null order 1.999999; 10¹⁰ s = 317 yr; the 0.9557 binomial coverage; Table 2.3's catalog inventory; Table 4.1 in full; Tables 5.1/5.3/B.2 in full; all sixteen numbers in Sec. 5.4; the 1760/2260 and 1320/1530 local-zodiacal fractions and the 184 → 0.011 leakage suppression.

## 4. Best achievement

The analytic reformulation of the circularly symmetric background response, together with the local-zodiacal normalisation correction it exposed and the amplification lesson drawn from it. The candidate derived a general even-order azimuthal average from scratch (Eq. 4.2 and App. A.1 — correct, including the subtle ½ from the imaging cosine), extended it to uniform-disk and tapered-radial sources, used it to remove image size as a production-science parameter, and in doing so found a 27.3 % normalisation defect in inherited code that had gone unnoticed because two branches of the same routine disagreed. The candidate then did the harder thing: rather than reporting the correction as a fix, quantified that a 27.3 % error in one background term moved the derived requirement by factors of 1.5 to 11 (Table 4.1), reproduced the same amplification with a 10⁻⁴ model perturbation (App. B.3), and turned it into a methodological warning that outlives the study — that iso-mission-time requirements must be quoted with the background model that produced them, and that a background correction invalidates rather than perturbs them. That is a genuine, transferable scientific result, and it is the strongest evidence in the thesis of scientific competence in the sense this criterion means.

## 5. Opportunity for improvement

Apply the scepticism of Sec. 4.2 to Sec. 5.7. The architecture comparison is the thesis's most advertised contribution and is the one place where its own standards slip: the equal-area convention is declared but not implemented in the field of view (E4), the idealized and realizable fourth-order models are interleaved in the Discussion (E2, E3), and the statistics that explain the result have no provenance (E10). The fix for the first is one line — recompute `hfov` from the per-collector diameter implied by the equal-area convention and rerun — and it is the single most valuable experiment left undone, because it could reverse the headline. The fix for the second is a column heading. Separately, the literature base should roughly double: a thesis on nulling error budgets that never cites Bracewell (1978), Angel & Woolf (1997) — whose two architectures it studies by name — or the Darwin/TPF-I budget literature beyond a single Lay citation is under-read for its own subject, and this both weakens the positioning and, in the case of the four-aperture counting argument (E6), leaves a claim stated more generally than the literature would have permitted.

## 6. Suggested grade

### **5.25**

**Justification against the scale wording.** *"5.5 — very good, only minor flaws"* is not available, because at least two flaws are not minor. E4 is an inconsistency between the comparison convention the thesis declares and the model it runs, sitting directly under the result the abstract and conclusion lead with, and it is large enough to plausibly reverse that result's sign; it is also absent from an otherwise exemplary limitations table. E3 misstates the engineering headroom bought by a deeper null by a factor of ≈4.6 in the Discussion, using an allowance the thesis itself disowns two pages earlier. Alongside these sit an internal contradiction about far-field averages (E2), a precision claim attached to the wrong quantity (E5), an over-general theorem statement (E6), a robustness claim inside its own error bar (E8), a duplicated paragraph with contradictory magnitudes in the delivered PDF (E1), and a literature base of fifteen references missing the founding papers of the very architectures under study.

*"5 — good, certain flaws"* understates the work. Every derivation I checked independently is correct, including two that are easy to get wrong. Roughly twenty distinct numerical claims spanning six tables and a full results section reproduce **exactly** from raw catalogs and reproducibility files — a standard of traceability I would accept in a refereed paper. The validation matrix (Table 3.2) and the ranked limitations table (Table 6.1) discriminate between what a check establishes and what it cannot, which is the specific competence this criterion measures, and the candidate volunteers unflattering findings (a factor-of-several overstatement in an earlier configuration; a reporting error in the candidate's own Table 5.1) rather than concealing them. The aims are crisply posed and answered one by one, the methods are appropriate and correctly bounded, the sensitivity coverage is broad, and the outlook is concrete and follows from the stated limitations.

The work therefore sits between the two descriptors: better than "good with certain flaws" on derivation, validation, provenance and self-criticism; short of "only minor flaws" on the correctness of its headline architecture comparison and on literature breadth. **5.25** places it there. It would move to 5.5 with the field-of-view correction of E4 either applied or explicitly ranked as a limitation, the proxy/realizable labelling of E2/E3 repaired, and the reference list broadened to the founding literature of the architectures studied.
